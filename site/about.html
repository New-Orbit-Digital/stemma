About Stemma

Stemma is a community-maintained catalog of strain lineage and history, for hobbyists: where a cannabis variety came from, when it emerged, and what the sources say.

What this is

Each strain gets one card. A card records the parents, the rough date and place it emerged, the breeder where there is one, and the list of sources it was built from. Cards link to their parents and their children, so you can walk a family back toward the landraces it came from.

Think of it as a library catalog rather than a verdict. The aim is to be honest rather than exhaustive: “unknown” is a valid answer, dates are often decade-precision, and a strain we have not researched yet shows up as a stub so a lineage can still end somewhere. Everything is written in our own words.

How sources are categorized

Every card ends with the sources behind it, each tagged with one of four categories. The categories say what kind of thing a source is. They are descriptive labels, not a ranking, and nothing on the site treats one as outranking another.

breeder
A breeder or seed company. Often the only account of a cross there is, and not independent of the people selling it.
publication
Books, journals, and press.
database
Strain databases and encyclopedias.
community
Blogs, forums, and online magazines.

Read them together. A breeder page and a forum thread that agree are worth more than either alone, and a category tells you what to discount for, not whether to believe it.

Where accounts differ

Strain history is oral history for the most part, and the accounts often disagree. When they do, the card says so in its summary, in plain prose: which accounts exist, and which one the parents reflect. There is no separate verdict to look up, and a disagreement is not a flaw in a card. It is usually the most interesting thing on it.

Why indica and sativa are traditional labels

Cards show a traditional label — indica, sativa, hybrid, or unknown — because that is how these plants have been described in catalogs, shops, and grower lore for decades. It is a label from that history, not a measurement.

Decades of crossing mean almost everything in circulation is a hybrid, and the label tracks how a variety has been marketed and described far better than it tracks what is in the plant. Treat it as a piece of a strain’s story, not as a prediction of how it grows or how it will feel.

Not medical advice

Stemma is a history and reference project for adults 21 and over. Nothing here is medical advice, and nothing here is a claim about what a strain will do for you. Talk to a clinician about anything health-related, and follow the law where you live.
