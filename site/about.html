About Stemma

Stemma is a strain-lineage catalog: where a cannabis variety came from, when it emerged, and who says so.

What this is

Each strain gets one card. A card records the parents, the rough date and place it emerged, the breeder where there is one, and a source for every claim. Cards link to their parents and their children, so you can walk a family back toward the landraces it came from.

The catalog aims to be honest rather than exhaustive. “Unknown” is a valid answer, dates are often decade-precision, and a strain we have not researched yet shows up as a stub so a lineage can still end somewhere. Everything is written in our own words.

The four evidence tiers

Every claim carries a badge naming the strongest kind of evidence behind it. Strongest first:

genetically tested
DNA evidence, such as a genotype report. Important caveat: a genetic test verifies the sample that was tested, not the name that sample was sold under. Two packs bought under the same name can be different plants, and a test on one of them says nothing about the other. Where we know it, the card notes which sample was tested.
documented
Contemporaneous or published records: books, magazines, interviews, seed catalogs.
breeder claimed
The breeder’s own account of what they crossed. Often the only account there is, and not independent.
folklore
Community lore with nothing stronger behind it. Worth recording, and labeled as such.

Why indica and sativa are traditional labels

Cards show a traditional label — indica, sativa, hybrid, or unknown — because that is how these plants have been described in catalogs, shops, and grower lore for decades. It is a label from that history, not a measurement.

Decades of crossing mean almost everything in circulation is a hybrid, and the label tracks how a variety has been marketed and described far better than it tracks what is in the plant. Treat it as a piece of a strain’s story, not as a prediction of how it grows or how it will feel.

How to read disputes

When credible accounts disagree about a strain’s parents, the card records the disagreement instead of picking a winner. The Lineage section shows the best-supported account. A Disputed block below it lists each competing claim, the parents it names, and its own evidence tier.

A dispute is not a flaw in the card. It is usually the most interesting thing on it.

Not medical advice

Stemma is a history and reference project for adults 21 and over. Nothing here is medical advice, and nothing here is a claim about what a strain will do for you. Talk to a clinician about anything health-related, and follow the law where you live.
